Over the years, many things become calmer. Favourite spots get warmer, play sessions get shorter. Older cats often show changes only quietly: they drink a little more, jump onto their favourite spot less often or groom themselves less thoroughly. If you notice such small things, you can mention them specifically to your vet.

This article does not replace veterinary advice. For anything unusual, your vet is the right person to ask.

## What you can observe

- **Drinking and eating:** How much, and how eagerly?
- **Movement:** Does she still jump onto her favourite spots, or does she need help?
- **Grooming:** Is she grooming as usual?
- **Sleep and rest:** Has her rhythm changed?
- **Mood:** Is she as approachable as before?

## Regular rather than random

A short note once a month is enough. That way you recognise changes over time that you might otherwise miss in daily life, and at the next appointment you can report specifically what you have observed.

## Making everyday life more comfortable

Small adjustments can help: a low step up to a favourite spot, a soft blanket in a warm place, food and water within easy reach.
